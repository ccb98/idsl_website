Optical Oscilloscope: real-time volumetric voltage imaging

Active

Wellcome · with the Department of Bioengineering

Imaging the electrical activity of neurons across a whole network of brain tissue, in three dimensions and in real time, using light-field microscopy. Scattering in brain tissue and the heavy computation behind the method are the main obstacles. We build optics-aware, computationally efficient deep neural networks that recover neuronal voltage signals from scattered light fields, and implement them on FPGAs and GPUs for kilohertz read-out suitable for closed-loop experiments.

  • FunderWellcome (£3.92M award)
  • PartnersBioengineering and UK DRI at Imperial; led by Dr Amanda Foust
  • Our roleProf. Christos Bouganis, co-investigator: neural networks on FPGAs and GPUs

APRIL AI Hub: AI for Productive Research & Innovation in eLectronics

Active · since 2024

UKRI · EPSRC AI Hub, grant EP/Y029763/1

APRIL is a national centre of excellence that brings the electronics and AI communities together to develop and commercialise AI tools that raise productivity across the semiconductor and electronics supply chain. Its research is organised into five pillars, from materials discovery to testing and modelling. Pillar 3, Circuit & System Design, brings AI into commercial circuit design tools to generate new circuit topologies and system architectures faster, and to adapt known designs to new technologies. A major theme is a large-language-model toolchain that turns natural-language specifications into analogue, digital and RF circuit designs inside commercial design automation tools, guided by power, performance and area feedback.

  • FunderUKRI AI programme and EPSRC
  • HubLed from the University of Edinburgh; 20 academic partners and over 30 industrial members
  • Our roleProf. Christos Bouganis co-leads Pillar 3 (Circuit & System Design) with Prof. Michael O'Boyle, University of Edinburgh

HAMLET: human-centred generative AI for cultural industries

Active · 2025–2027

European Union · Horizon Europe, grant agreement 101178362

HAMLET makes generative AI tools available to cultural and creative organisations of any size, so they can take part in the digital transition. The project builds a collaboration hub where artists, designers, game developers and cultural professionals share expertise and resources, along with a suite of AI tools for areas such as 3D asset generation, adaptive storytelling and dance motion analysis. The tools are tested in pilot cases in several European countries.

  • FunderEuropean Union (EU contribution of about €3.9M)
  • Consortium12 organisations across Europe, including three from the UK, coordinated by a national research centre in Greece (CERTH)

An AI-ISP Component for Superior Image Quality to Enhance Edge Vision Systems

Active · 1 Aug 2026–31 Jan 2028 · 18 months

Innovate UK · led by Heronic Technologies with Imperial College London

AI-ISP is developing IRIS-ISP, a silicon-agnostic, AI-based image signal processor that cleans raw sensor data in hardware before it reaches the main GPU. By improving video quality in challenging lighting and sensor conditions, the project aims to strengthen downstream edge-AI detection.

Led by Heronic Technologies with Imperial, the project will deliver IRIS-ISP in an Altera Agilex 5 FPGA-based Rapid Deployment Kit. Imperial is developing middleware with NVIDIA Holoscan Sensor Bridge for deterministic video streaming. The initial target is high-speed optical inspection in manufacturing, with further applications in UAVs, UGVs and robotics.

  • Lead partnerHeronic Technologies
  • Imperial roleOptimised middleware integration with NVIDIA Holoscan Sensor Bridge
